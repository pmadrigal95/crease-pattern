I'm Pablo Madrigal, a systems engineer and Lead Frontend Architect in Costa Rica. I build the shared pieces
behind web products (components, tests and release pipelines) so teams can ship new features fast without
breaking what already works. This is the first post of Precrease, the part of my site where I write down how
that work gets done.

I started in 2015, studying information technology in Cartago. Two years later, as an intern, I delivered
most of a team's web application in Node.js and Express. Since 2019 I've built with Vue: first full
stack on .NET Core APIs, then at a product company where I joined as the only frontend developer and grew into
leading its frontend architecture.

## Why a crease pattern

In origami, the finished fox is what everyone sees. Unfold it and you get a square of paper covered in lines:
mountain folds, valley folds, the whole plan that made the fox possible. That sheet is called the crease
pattern.

Software has the same two layers. The interface is the fox. The architecture underneath is the crease pattern,
and it decides whether the next change takes an afternoon or a month. I built this site to show both. On the
home page, "See how this page is built" opens the Inspector: the page tilts into its layers, each part shows
the design values it uses and when its code last changed, and the fox unfolds into its creases. It works on
this post too.

## Why Precrease

Before you fold anything, you precrease: you mark the lines on the flat sheet so the real folds land where they
should. Nobody photographs that part, and it decides how the model turns out.

That is what this blog is about. Precrease covers software development in general, from architecture and
frontend to backend, systems, delivery and AI. The common thread is the work that happens before something takes
its shape, and an honest account of what happened after.

## Learning in public

Most of what I use today I taught myself, because a problem needed it. That habit gets things built, and it also
leaves gaps: classics I've quoted without reading them through, and basics I learned in the wrong order.

Precrease is where I fill those gaps in the open. Some posts will reread a classic and ask what still holds now
that a lot of code is written next to an AI assistant. Others will go back to fundamentals I wish someone had
explained to me earlier. I'll write about them as I get to them, without a fixed list.

## What you'll find here

Posts come in four formats:

- **Essays** are long reads, often with something to try. The first one will run a puzzle solver on real threads
  in your browser, and show why the naive version I wrote while learning concurrency didn't scale.
- **Notes** are short: one idea, one example.
- **Dog-ear** posts reread a book chapter by chapter: the idea in my own words, a verdict for today with a real
  example, what changes with AI, and a short quote with a link to the book.
- **Basic Folds** is a series on engineering fundamentals for people who build with AI and want solid footing
  before they prompt.

You can [subscribe](/precrease/subscribe) to the whole blog or to a single topic.

## The rules I write by

I don't invent numbers. When I show a metric, it is measured, and the code that measured it is in the
repository. When something fails, I show the failure too, because that is usually where the lesson is. Code and
examples are real, and nothing confidential from any employer ends up here.
