I'm Pablo Madrigal. I live in Costa Rica and work remotely as Lead Frontend Architect at Buo, an AI people
analytics platform for companies in Mexico and Latin America. My work is the shared foundation behind a web product: the components, the tests and the release
pipeline that let a team ship new features without breaking what already works. This is the first post of
Precrease, the part of my site where I write down how that work gets done.

I started in 2015, studying information technology in Cartago. My first professional project came two years
later, as an intern, working on an inherited web system in Node.js and Express. Since 2019 I've worked mostly
with Vue: first as a full stack developer on .NET Core APIs, then at Buo, where I joined in 2021 as the only
frontend developer and, as the team grew, became Frontend Lead and later Lead Frontend Architect.

## Why a crease pattern

In origami, the finished fox is what people see. Unfold it and you get a square of paper covered in lines:
mountain folds, valley folds, the plan that made the fox possible. That sheet is called the crease pattern.

Software has the same two layers. The interface is the fox. The architecture underneath is the crease pattern,
and it usually decides whether the next change takes an afternoon or a month. This site tries to show both. On
the home page, "See how this page is built" opens the Inspector: the page tilts into its layers, each part shows
the design values it uses and when its code last changed, and the fox unfolds into its creases. It works on
this post too.

## Why Precrease

Before folding, you precrease: you mark the lines on the flat sheet so the real folds land where they should.
It is the quiet part of the process, and much of the result depends on it.

That is what this blog is about. Precrease covers software development in general, from architecture and
frontend to backend, systems, delivery and AI. The common thread is the work that happens before something takes
its shape, and an honest account of what happened after.

## Learning in public

A good part of what I know I learned on the job, when a project called for it. That is a common path, and it
leaves its own gaps: books I know by reputation more than by reading, and fundamentals I picked up out of order.

Precrease is a place to work on those gaps calmly and in the open. Some posts will revisit a classic and ask what
still holds now that much of our code is written next to an AI assistant. Others will return to fundamentals
that are easy to skip and worth knowing well. I'll write about them as they come up, without a fixed list.

## What you'll find here

Posts come in four formats:

- **Essays** are long reads, often with something to try. The first one will run a puzzle solver on real threads
  in your browser, and look at why the first version I wrote while learning concurrency didn't scale.
- **Notes** are short: one idea, one example.
- **Dog-ear** posts revisit a book chapter by chapter: the idea in my own words, how it holds up today with a real
  example, what changes with AI, and a short quote with a link to the book.
- **Basic Folds** is a series on engineering fundamentals for people who build with AI and want solid footing
  before they prompt.

You can [subscribe](/precrease/subscribe) to the whole blog or to a single topic.

## How I write here

Numbers on this site are measured, and the code that measures them is in the repository. When something doesn't
work, I show that part too, because that is usually where the lesson is. Code and examples are real, and
nothing confidential from any employer appears here.
